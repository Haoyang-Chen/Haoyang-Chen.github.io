Editable XAI

Rewrite the rules behind an AI decision.

CoExplain lets people express their knowledge as decision rules, then work with AI to refine the thresholds and structure. The explanation becomes a way to shape the model—and understand how it changes.

43 participants · 3 domains

Editing improved understanding over read-only explanations. CoExplain reduced mean editing time from 7.18 to 3.36 minutes compared with manual editing.

Haoyang Chen, Jingwen Bai, Tian Fang, Brian Y. Lim
National University of Singapore

01 / Guided interface tour

Your rules. An AI proposal. Your decision.

Follow the house-price example to see what people can change, what AI assistance adds, and how the two are compared.

01 / Write

Put your reasoning into the model.

The user’s tree starts with grade > 9, then checks living area. Following the upper path, a house with living area > 2000 receives a high-price prediction.

These are editable rules on familiar attributes. CoExplain parses the written tree into a neural network, so the rules guide the model’s predictions.

What you controlAttributes, thresholds, and branches

02 / Enhance

Choose how much help you need.

Enhance Values refines numeric thresholds while keeping the tree’s structure. Enhance Flowchart can also reorganize the rules, adding or rearranging decision nodes.

Someone confident in the logic can ask for narrower adjustments; someone reconsidering the logic can allow structural changes.

Two kinds of assistanceRefine the numbers · Rework the structure

03 / Constrain

Keep the proposal close to your intent.

Prediction Similarity encourages the enhanced model to preserve the current tree’s predictions. Structure Similarity encourages it to retain the current arrangement of rules.

The edit history records updates, insertions, and removals. People can inspect what changed before accepting a proposal.

What the constraints doBalance learning from data with preserving rules

04 / Compare

Better performance still needs your judgment.

The proposed tree begins with living area, while the original begins with grade. Side-by-side rules and accuracy indicators make that change visible.

Users can accept the proposal or selectively incorporate parts they agree with. A separate simulation tests the rules on a test dataset.

02 / How it works

An explanation that connects back to the model.

CoExplain connects symbolic decision rules and a trainable neural network. Each interaction has a corresponding model operation.

Read / Distillation

Make predictions inspectable.

A decision tree learns to reproduce the neural network’s predictions, giving people an explanation in terms of attributes and thresholds.

Write / Parsing

Turn a person’s rules into a model.

The authored tree is mapped into a neural network: decision tests, paths, and outcomes become connected computations.

Enhance / Constrained learning

Learn while referring back to the rules.

Training improves fit to data while regularization encourages similar predictions and tree structure. The updated model is distilled into a tree for inspection.

A distilled tree approximates the network. The paper separately evaluates explanation–model faithfulness (§6.3.7); readability alone does not establish that the two behave identically.

03 / Study findings

Understanding through authoring.

43 participants were assigned to Read-only explanations, manual editing, or CoExplain. Each worked on income, house-price, and heart-disease prediction tasks, using supplied guideline rules as background knowledge.

The tradeoff

Aligning with a person can change predictive performance.

Manual editing produced rules closest to the supplied guidelines, but lowered accuracy on the model’s original distribution. CoExplain recovered part of that accuracy while retaining closer rule alignment than Read-only.

ConditionAccuracy ↑Rule distance ↓

Rule distance is Tree Edit Distance to the supplied guideline tree; lower means closer structure. These tasks deliberately used different guideline and pre-trained data distributions. Source: §6.3.3–6.3.5, Figure 8.

Scope of the evidence. A controlled study with university participants across three prediction tasks. The results concern understanding, alignment to supplied guidelines, and editing effort; they do not establish clinical or other real-world deployment outcomes.
